# Neighborhood Laundromat Website Plan

## Plan boundary

This document defines the creative direction and experience architecture only. It does not prescribe a framework, dependencies, rendering technique, or implementation scaffolding. The business name, services, pricing, hours, equipment, policies, and neighborhood recommendations must come from the owner or verified sources before launch.

## 1. Request contract

### Known

- The business is a small neighborhood laundromat.
- The desired website should be unusually ambitious and memorable.
- The owner is not expected to arrive with design or website expertise.
- The immediate deliverable is a plan for review, not a build.

### Provisional planning assumptions

- Most visitors arrive with a practical intent: decide whether to visit, confirm the laundromat can handle their load, or plan the trip.
- The business serves local residents more often than destination visitors.
- A mobile phone is the dominant context, often used while carrying laundry or already en route.
- The laundromat may offer self-service washing and drying. Drop-off, pickup, delivery, payment methods, and live machine availability remain unconfirmed.

### Unknowns to resolve before content design

- Business name, address, phone number, hours, holiday hours, and accessibility details
- Machine quantities, capacities, cycle durations, prices, and payment methods
- Confirmed services, policies, amenities, staff coverage, and last-load time
- Whether live machine availability or online service ordering exists
- Brand assets and permission to photograph customers, staff, and the premises
- Nearby places that are safe and appropriate to recommend while a cycle runs
- Languages used by the neighborhood

### Visitor jobs

1. Find the address, open/closed status, hours, directions, and contact details immediately.
2. Understand services, machine sizes, prices, payment methods, cycle timing, and policies.
3. Decide what kind of load to bring and what the visit will involve.
4. Use an available ordering or availability feature without navigating through the expressive experience.
5. Feel that the laundromat is clean, dependable, welcoming, and part of the neighborhood.

## 2. Domain excavation

- **Functional truth:** mixed, worn items move through sorting, washing, drying, folding, and return as a usable whole.
- **Visitor reality:** people arrive carrying weight and uncertainty; they want a predictable result, a clear cost, and confidence that the right machine is available.
- **Human ritual:** laundry creates an interval between loading and unloading—a recurring pause people fill with errands, conversation, reading, food, or quiet.
- **Material behavior:** tumbling, gathering, separating, saturation, repetition, circular timing, lint, fabric texture, enamel, stainless steel, perforated drums, labels, baskets, and folded edges.
- **Cultural tension:** laundromats are usually presented as anonymous utility, yet they are one of the few shared rooms where a neighborhood repeatedly performs private domestic work in public.
- **Native language:** load, cycle, last load, wash, rinse, spin, dry, fold, delicate, heavy, capacity, remaining time.

The obvious first association is a giant animated washer door in a blue-and-white site. It is retained only as raw material; a machine-shaped spectacle would be literal, interchangeable, and less useful than a concept built around the human interval the machine creates.

## 3. Candidate directions

### A. While It Turns — selected

- **Proposition:** The laundromat gives a useful interval back to the visitor while the machines do their work.
- **Domain truth:** every load creates a bounded period of waiting, and that period belongs to the person and the surrounding neighborhood.
- **Promise:** arrive prepared, understand the visit, and see the cycle as claimed time rather than lost time.
- **Structure:** hybrid utility journey with a concise editorial neighborhood layer.
- **Visual world:** documentary neighborhood photography, tactile fabric fields, machine-label precision, and time expressed as measured bands rather than clocks everywhere.
- **Motion law:** loose things gather, rotate once with purpose, then settle into an orderly state.
- **Signature interaction:** “Plan your interval.” A visitor chooses a verified load or service type; the experience unfolds a simple trip timeline showing preparation, estimated on-site stages, cost guidance, and appropriate ways to use the interval. It always ends with directions or the relevant service action.
- **Utility integration:** hours, address, open status, directions, phone, and pricing remain fixed near the top and do not require the interaction.
- **Ownability:** removing laundry removes the real timed interval, load stages, machine capacities, and transformation on which the entire experience depends.

### B. The Care Ledger

- **Proposition:** Present laundry as a quiet record of how a neighborhood takes care of daily life.
- **Structure:** editorial composition centered on real garments, care labels, staff knowledge, and short local stories.
- **Strength:** warm, distinctive, and capable of elevating an overlooked service.
- **Reason not selected:** it depends on excellent original reporting and permissions, and it is less directly useful to a visitor planning a load.

### C. The Clean Slate

- **Proposition:** Every visit converts visual and practical disorder into a fresh start.
- **Structure:** transformation-led utility journey in which scattered content resolves into ordered information.
- **Strength:** immediately legible and visually powerful.
- **Reason not selected:** “reset” language and before/after transformation are common enough to weaken ownership of the concept.

### D. The Neighborhood Drum

- **Proposition:** The repeating drum becomes a portrait of the neighborhood’s shared rhythm.
- **Structure:** spatial, circular exploration with utility anchored around the perimeter.
- **Strength:** iconic and high-impact.
- **Reason not selected:** it inherits the category’s first visual association and risks making navigation serve the shape of a washer.

Evidence for the top scores: the timed interval is a real consequence of a laundry cycle; the signature interaction turns verified timing, load, and pricing data into a trip plan; and the same premise governs content, motion, utility, and neighborhood material. ## 5. Selected concept contract

### Governing concept

**While the machines turn, the neighborhood opens up.**

This is an experience concept, not a proposed business rename or slogan.

### Experience promise

The visitor should understand the visit in seconds, feel that their time is respected, and leave with a clear plan. The emotional shift is from “a chore that consumes part of my day” to “a predictable interval I can use.”

### Experience structure

A **hybrid utility journey** surrounds one expressive planning interaction:

1. **Immediate answer strip:** business name, current open status based on verified hours, next closing or opening time, address, directions, and contact.
2. **Opening statement:** the concept in one short line, paired with a direct “Plan a load” action and a quieter “See prices and machines” action.
3. **Plan your interval:** the signature interaction, using only verified load types, cycle estimates, services, and prices.
4. **What fits:** a plain-language capacity guide with real examples such as everyday clothing or bedding, mapped to the actual machines.
5. **Use the interval:** approved on-site amenities first; nearby suggestions only when policy, safety, distance, and local business details are confirmed.
6. **The room:** honest photographs of the entrance, machines, seating, payment station, folding area, staff, and accessibility conditions so a first visit feels predictable.
7. **Practical details:** full hours, last-load policy, prices, payment methods, services, accessibility, FAQs, and any ordering or availability action.
8. **Arrival panel:** map, transit/parking notes, phone number, and a final directions action.

The site never forces a visitor to finish a story, rotate a dial, or wait for motion before reaching practical information.

## 6. Art direction

### Composition

- Use a measured editorial grid whose bands resemble a cycle schedule or care-label system without imitating a control panel.
- Alternate dense, precise utility rows with large fields of fabric and neighborhood imagery; ambition comes from contrast and pacing, not from filling every region.
- Let the planning interval span the page as the visual centerpiece. All other sections remain calm enough to make it singular.
- Use folded edges, perforation patterns, basket-like containment, and seams sparingly as structural cues rather than decoration.

### Typography

- A confident, compact display face gives the concept a civic, neighborhood voice rather than a luxury-appliance tone.
- A highly legible workhorse sans handles prices, timings, directions, machine capacity, and policies.
- A tabular numeral style makes time, price, and capacity easy to compare.
- Language stays plain and concrete: “last load,” “fits a comforter,” “takes approximately…,” and “pay with…” once those facts are verified.

### Material and color

- Base colors come from the actual space after photography: warm fabric white, stainless gray, and a dark ink color.
- Select one high-visibility operational color from a real shop detail—signage, baskets, machine labels, or staff clothing—for open status and primary actions.
- Reserve a second color for time-in-progress states only. Do not default to generic detergent blue or decorative gradients.
- Texture should come from real cotton, knit, lint-screen mesh, enamel, and metal rather than simulated futuristic surfaces.

### Imagery

- Commission an honest documentary set in the real laundromat and its immediate block.
- Prioritize hands loading, sorting and folding; varied load sizes; the doorway from the street; staff at work; and clear spatial views useful to first-time visitors.
- Photograph real people only with explicit releases. If customers cannot be used, center staff, objects, and unoccupied moments rather than stock models.
- Avoid spotless appliance-advertising imagery, staged detergent splashes, and generic neighborhood claims.

## 7. Motion and signature interaction

### Motion law

Elements begin slightly ungrouped, follow one measured turn or lateral cycle, and settle into alignment. Nothing floats indefinitely. Repetition communicates predictable service; settling communicates the completed task.

### “Plan your interval” states

1. **Rest:** essential visit details and a small set of confirmed load/service choices are visible.
2. **Choose:** the visitor selects the closest load or service type using ordinary controls.
3. **Unfold:** the relevant stages expand into a readable timeline with verified estimates and cost guidance.
4. **Claim:** approved on-site activities or nearby options occupy the genuinely free portion of the interval.
5. **Act:** the visitor gets directions, calls, checks availability, or begins an order if that capability exists.
6. **Recover:** changing or clearing the selection is immediate; browser navigation and refresh never trap the visitor in a state.

Secondary movement is limited to status changes, section entrances, and the gather-and-settle behavior. There is no custom cursor, scroll hijacking, decorative particle field, or continuously spinning washer.

### Sound

No ambient or automatic sound. The website is likely to be used in public, on the move, or inside a working laundromat; sound adds little to the visitor’s task and would compete with the concept’s clarity.

## 8. Responsive and accessible expression

- **Mobile:** the answer strip becomes a compact sticky utility bar; the interval becomes a vertical sequence readable with one thumb; directions and phone actions remain prominent.
- **Touch:** choices use clear tap targets and never depend on dragging or precise circular gestures.
- **Keyboard:** every choice, state change, reveal, reset, and primary action follows a logical focus order and has a visible focus state.
- **Reduced motion:** the timeline changes through immediate layout and restrained fades; the same before/after organization remains without rotation or travel.
- **Low capability:** all content, prices, hours, policies, and actions remain ordinary readable page content. The concept survives through composition, language, and state labels.
- **Semantics:** operational details use real headings, lists, tables, addresses, and action labels. Status is never communicated by color alone.
- **Language access:** if the neighborhood uses multiple languages, language selection belongs in the persistent utility area and all operational content receives human-reviewed translations.

## 9. Deliberate restraint

The launch should exclude a giant 3D washer, simulated soap bubbles, particles, cinematic scroll chapters, auto-playing video, a custom cursor, invented customer stories, decorative live counters, and unverified machine availability. It should also avoid turning every section into a card. One strong planning interaction, real photography, and unusually precise utility are enough.

## 10. Content and approval checklist

Before visual design begins, the owner should approve or provide:

- Complete operating facts and service list
- Actual machine capacities, cycle ranges, prices, and payment methods
- Policy on leaving loads and the boundary for neighborhood suggestions
- Accessibility, parking, transit, entrance, and safety information
- The primary conversion action: directions, call, availability, or order
- Photography access, releases, and any existing brand assets
- Required languages and who will verify translations

Once those inputs are available, the next review should be a content-complete wireframe of the immediate answer strip, the “Plan your interval” states, and the mobile utility path. Art direction should proceed only after that utility path is approved.

## 11. Acceptance criteria for the eventual design

- A first-time mobile visitor can find hours, location, directions, contact, pricing, and payment information within ten seconds.
- The site remains complete when motion is reduced and when the signature interaction is skipped.
- Every time, price, availability state, service, and neighborhood recommendation is verified or clearly labeled as an estimate.
- The “Plan your interval” interaction changes the visitor’s understanding or plan; it is not decorative animation.
- The experience can be recalled as “the laundromat that helps me claim the time between loads,” without mentioning an effect or technology.
- Replacing the laundromat with an unrelated business would break the content structure, motion law, language, and signature interaction.
