# Blind Evaluation Rubric

Score each response from 1 to 5 on every dimension. Judge decisions, not length, confidence, formatting, or the number of effects proposed.

| Dimension | 1 | 3 | 5 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Governing concept | No unifying idea | A theme connects some choices | One precise premise governs the whole experience |
| Domain insight | Could describe any business | Uses familiar laundromat associations | Finds a specific human or cultural truth about the place |
| Originality | Recombines common creative-web tropes | Contains a few non-obvious choices | Produces a surprising direction that still feels inevitable |
| Conceptual coherence | A list of sections and effects | Mostly connected direction | Content, layout, motion, sound, and interaction express the same idea |
| Interaction necessity | Effects are decorative | Some interactions support meaning | Signature interaction could only belong to this concept |
| Narrative progression | Conventional page stack | Has a loose journey | Experience changes state with deliberate setup, development, and payoff |
| Art-direction specificity | Mood adjectives only | Identifies usable visual ingredients | Makes concrete, ownable choices about type, material, image, color, rhythm, and composition |
| Discernment and restraint | Adds everything available | Prioritizes a few devices | Explicitly chooses what carries the idea and rejects attractive distractions |
| Practical intelligence | Ignores the actual business | Includes core visitor needs | Ambition and utility reinforce each other without becoming a gimmick |
| Skill independence | Copies obvious skill examples | Adapts some skill patterns | Uses the method while inventing a subject-specific visual and interaction language |

## Evidence notes

For every score of 1 or 5, quote or paraphrase one concrete decision as evidence. Then record:

- the plan's governing concept in one sentence;
- its signature interaction;
- the three most memorable decisions;
- any motif apparently inherited from the skill examples;
- whether the plan would still make sense if “laundromat” were replaced with another noun.

## Red flags

- “Cinematic,” “immersive,” or “award-winning” substitutes for an actual idea.
- The answer inventories Three.js, particles, shaders, smooth scroll, and a custom cursor without explaining why they belong.
- It follows `hero -> about -> services -> gallery -> contact` with motion added afterward.
- It copies the skill's void, crystalline fragments, dust typography, dramatic metrics, destruction/reconstruction, or protected-reveal sequence without earning them from the brief.
- It mistakes maximal technical complexity for ambition.
- It forgets prices, hours, location, machine availability, accessibility, or the reason a laundromat customer visits.

## Overall judgment

After scoring, answer two separate questions:

1. Is this model a strong creative director from sparse input?
2. Is this model a strong executor once another agent supplies the creative direction?

Do not collapse those into one verdict.
