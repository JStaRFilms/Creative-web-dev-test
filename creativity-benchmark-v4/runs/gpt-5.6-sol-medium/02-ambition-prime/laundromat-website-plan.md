# Website Plan — The Neighborhood Clock

## 1. Request Contract

**Deliverable:** a creative direction and experience architecture for a small neighborhood laundromat website.

**Stopping point:** concept and experience architecture only. This plan intentionally does not choose a framework, dependencies, rendering method, data architecture, performance budget, or implementation sequence.

**Governing concept:** **The laundromat becomes the neighborhood clock: every turn of the cycle helps someone get a piece of life back in order.**

**Experience promise:** Within seconds, a visitor can answer the practical questions that determine a visit. If they stay, the same information unfolds into a warm, memorable portrait of laundry as a shared neighborhood rhythm rather than an anonymous chore.

### Known facts

- The subject is a small neighborhood laundromat.
- The owner wants an unusually ambitious website.
- The website should be planned before it is built.

### Provisional creative assumptions

- The brand should feel useful, human, and local rather than luxurious or corporate.
- The physical experience contains repeating cycles, turning drums, fabric, water, waiting, folding, and return.
- Most visitors will arrive with practical intent and limited patience.

These assumptions guide the art direction only. They are not customer-facing claims about the actual business.

### Unknown content requirements

The following must be supplied or verified before customer-facing copy is written:

- Business name, logo, address, map destination, phone number, and contact preference
- Opening hours and holiday exceptions
- Prices, payment methods, machine sizes, and machine count
- Confirmed services, such as self-service, wash-and-fold, pickup, or delivery
- Confirmed amenities, accessibility details, parking or transit guidance, policies, and last-wash time
- Whether machine availability or service status can be shown accurately
- Original photography, permission to photograph customers or staff, and any genuine business history

No service, price, amenity, live status, or neighborhood recommendation should appear as fact until confirmed.

## 2. Visitor Jobs and Utility Floor

The first screen must answer or provide a direct path to four questions:

1. **Where is it?** Show the verified address placeholder and a persistent directions action.
2. **When can I go?** Show verified hours placeholders, including any confirmed last-wash policy.
3. **Can it handle my laundry?** Summarize only verified machine capacities and services.
4. **What will it cost, and how do I pay?** Show only verified pricing and payment details.

A call or other confirmed contact action remains close at hand. None of these tasks may depend on completing animation, dragging a control, reading the brand story, enabling sound, or progressing through the full page.

## 3. Domain Excavation

### Functional truth

Laundry is a visible state change: separate, load, wash, dry, fold, return. Disorder becomes readiness through a finite sequence. The website should inherit this clarity rather than merely decorate itself with bubbles.

### Visitor reality

Visitors often carry a physical burden and a small set of urgent uncertainties. They need confidence before making the trip. The expressive experience must therefore begin with orientation, not suspense.

### Human ritual

Laundry is repetitive but intimate. It marks weeks, school days, work shifts, weather, children growing, and the reset before another round of life. A laundromat briefly synchronizes people whose routines would otherwise remain separate.

### Material behavior

Useful native behaviors include rotation, tumbling, soaking, weight settling, fabric folding, circular windows, indicator marks, care symbols, perforated metal, enamel, woven texture, and the decisive stop at the end of a cycle.

### Cultural tension

The category is often presented as purely transactional or visually neglected, even though the service safeguards the clothes, bedding, uniforms, and textiles through which daily life happens. The site can grant dignity to the errand without pretending it is a luxury ritual.

### Native language

Load, cycle, wash, dry, fold, delicate, heavy, rinse, spin, ready, open, last wash, capacity, time, and care. These words can structure navigation and hierarchy, but operational terms appear publicly only when they match verified business details.

## 4. Quarantined First Associations

The obvious answer is a giant animated washing-machine door, blue bubbles, rounded cards, and a clean aqua palette. The circular drum remains useful, but only after it is transformed into a **clock and public wayfinding instrument**. Bubbles, floating particles, wet-look gradients, novelty laundry icons, and a machine-shaped page are excluded.

Scores of 5 for the selected direction reflect concrete properties: its premise comes from finite wash cycles and weekly routines; replacing the laundromat with an unrelated business breaks the clock/cycle relationship; every visual and behavioral decision can register, turn, settle, and repeat; the dial is simultaneously the central interaction and the four-part utility index; and the practical questions are elevated rather than hidden. The Neighborhood Clock wins because its interaction is how visitors navigate the essential information, not an ornament applied around it.

## 6. Selected Concept Contract

### Structure

A **hybrid utility and editorial composition**: a permanent utility rail surrounds one expressive, cyclical journey. The site should feel complete after ten seconds but richer after two minutes.

The experience has two simultaneous layers:

- **The fixed facts:** name, verified open/closed wording if supportable, directions, contact, and the four-question utility index.
- **The turning day:** an editorial sequence that gives material and human meaning to wash, dry, fold, and return without inventing business history or customer stories.

### Composition

- Use a strong circular field as the page’s organizing geometry, intersected by a strict rectangular utility grid.
- Treat the circle as a measuring instrument with ticks, registration points, and calm central space—not as a cartoon appliance.
- Keep essential information aligned and still even when the expressive layer turns around it.
- Alternate dense bands of practical detail with large moments of visual quiet, echoing activity followed by a finished cycle.
- Let some headings follow an arc, but keep addresses, hours, prices, service details, and actions on straight lines for instant reading.
- Avoid a procession of interchangeable cards. Information should feel placed on one continuous instrument.

### Typography

- Pair a sturdy, highly legible public-signage voice for facts with a warmer, characterful display voice for the human rhythm.
- Use large numerals only for verified operational information or navigation markers; never invent impressive counts or durations for visual effect.
- Use short, plain labels: **WHERE / WHEN / WHAT / COST** until real brand copy is approved.
- Typographic movement may orbit, but words settle horizontally whenever they need to be read or acted upon.

### Material and color

- Begin with warm off-white, deep ink, stainless gray, and one saturated signal color chosen after the physical shop and existing identity are seen.
- Draw texture from woven fibers, enamel signs, drum perforations, stamped care marks, and the slight softness of repeatedly washed cloth.
- Keep surfaces tactile and honest. Do not use glossy futurism, luxury black, soap-blue gradients, or cleanliness clichés.
- The signal color marks what is actionable or currently selected; it should not wash over every surface.

### Imagery

- Prioritize original close observations: hands folding, fabric moving, a circular door, signage, baskets, machine controls, and the relationship between the storefront and its street.
- If people are photographed, use real staff or consenting participants and show ordinary action rather than staged delight.
- Do not use generic stock portraits as evidence of this neighborhood.
- Until original imagery exists, the plan should rely on typographic and material studies, not invented documentary content.

### Motion law

**Everything turns with purpose, finds its registration point, and becomes still enough to use.**

Movement should have a sense of weight. Elements do not float or scatter. They rotate, tumble briefly, align, fold, and rest. One settled state always precedes another movement, so the experience feels measured rather than continuously busy.

### Signature interaction: Turn the Cycle

The central dial has four registered positions: **WHERE, WHEN, WHAT, COST**. A visitor can turn or directly select it. Each position brings the matching verified information placeholder into the same stable reading zone, so the important answer does not chase the visitor around the circle.

The interaction earns its presence in three ways:

1. It turns the native cycle into a navigation model.
2. It makes practical certainty—not visual spectacle—the reward.
3. It teaches the larger motion law: turn, align, settle, continue.

Every position must also be available as a conventional visible link. On touch, the positions behave as generous direct targets. With a keyboard, focus advances predictably among the four choices. If motion is reduced, selection changes the registered content with a simple, immediate state change rather than rotation.

### Sound

Omit sound. Machine ambience would be intrusive, difficult to make useful, and likely to romanticize noise that customers already experience in person. The rhythm should be visible and tactile in behavior, not audible.

### Restraint

Deliberately exclude:

- Gamified washing, drag-and-drop clothing, bubbles, foam, particles, custom cursors, and novelty spin effects
- Auto-playing sound or video
- A long cinematic entrance before practical information
- Fabricated community statistics, testimonials, environmental claims, or live machine status
- Multiple competing interaction systems
- Decorative testimonials, articles, loyalty prompts, or newsletter sections unless the real business need and content exist

## 7. Experience Architecture

### State 1 — Immediate orientation: “Set your cycle”

The opening view pairs the unknown business name placeholder with the four-position utility dial. The verified address placeholder, hours placeholder, directions action, and contact action remain visible without operating the dial. A concise line introduces the concept without making a business claim: **“A familiar turn in the rhythm of the neighborhood.”** This is proposed brand language, not a factual assertion.

The composition should communicate small-business confidence: precise, cared for, and easy to understand. There is no splash screen and no demand to scroll.

### State 2 — WHERE: arrive with confidence

Show the verified address, map destination, storefront identification, and only confirmed parking, transit, entrance, or accessibility guidance. The editorial layer narrows from the larger circle to a clear point on the street: the cycle finds its place.

**Unknown requirement at point of use:** exact location details, map link, exterior photography, and access information.

### State 3 — WHEN: fit the visit into real life

Show verified opening hours, holiday handling, and last-wash information if the business actually uses one. The visual rhythm can mark a day without claiming live open status unless accurate ongoing data is available.

**Unknown requirement at point of use:** hours, exceptions, last-wash policy, and whether current status can be maintained reliably.

### State 4 — WHAT: choose the right kind of visit

Present confirmed machine capacities, supported load types, services, and relevant policies in plain language. Use fabric scale or folding proportions as an editorial aid only after the real machine and service details are known.

**Unknown requirement at point of use:** equipment sizes, services, accepted items, supplies, accessibility, and policies.

### State 5 — COST: remove the final uncertainty

Show verified prices and accepted payment methods together. The composition reaches its most settled state here: firm baselines, minimal movement, no promotional distraction. If pricing varies, explain the real rule plainly rather than replacing it with “from” language without context.

**Unknown requirement at point of use:** complete pricing, payment methods, taxes or fees if relevant, and the date or process for keeping information current.

### State 6 — The fold: one compact visit summary

End with a single, printable and easily shareable summary of the verified essentials: business name, address, hours, contact, services, payment, and directions. This is the page at rest—the information is folded into a usable form. No new persuasion or interaction is introduced at the end.

## 8. Responsive and Accessible Expression

### Small screens and touch

The dial becomes a compact four-stop index rather than a precision gesture. Direct taps are primary; swiping or turning is optional. The current answer occupies a stable region beneath it, and directions/contact remain within easy reach. Arc-set display text is reduced so practical text stays horizontal and comfortably sized.

### Keyboard

All four positions are ordinary focusable choices with visible focus states and meaningful labels. The selected state is announced, and focus does not jump when content changes. A skip path reaches the complete practical summary directly.

### Reduced motion

Preserve the clock concept through registration marks, sequence, and selection state. Replace rotation and tumbling with direct changes and brief emphasis. Nothing essential relies on motion to explain status or hierarchy.

### Low-capability or unenhanced expression

The experience becomes a well-composed single page with the same four-question index and the same order of information. The concept survives through circular framing, alignment, vocabulary, and the final “folded” summary. Utility remains complete.

### Readability and comprehension

Operational details stay horizontal, high-contrast, and written in plain language. Decorative arc text is never the sole copy. Symbols supplement words rather than replace them. The page structure uses clear landmarks and headings, and the signature interaction always has a direct-link equivalent.

## 9. What Must Remain Simple

- Directions, hours, pricing, services, payment, and contact
- The language used to explain policies or machine choices
- The number of primary actions
- The final visit summary
- The path for anyone who does not want the expressive interaction

The site should feel ambitious because one idea governs every layer—not because every section performs.

## 10. Primary Risk and Next Evidence

**Creative risk:** The circular system could regress into the first association—a large animated washer—or make a modest business feel overproduced. The safeguard is to prototype the opening view first as a utility instrument: if a visitor cannot find directions and hours immediately, or if the experience is remembered only as “the spinning site,” the direction has failed.

**Evidence needed before design is finalized:** a photographed walk-through of the storefront and interior; the complete verified operating details; the existing identity, if any; and a short owner interview about who visits, what customers ask most often, and what makes this laundromat genuinely particular. These inputs should sharpen the concept without manufacturing a community story.
