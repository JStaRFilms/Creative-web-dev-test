# Neighborhood Laundromat Website Plan

## 1. Request contract

**Deliverable:** a creative and experience plan for review. This stops at experience architecture; it does not choose a framework, prescribe dependencies, or describe implementation phases.

**Known from the brief**

- The business is a small neighborhood laundromat.
- The owner wants a website plan before anything is built.

**Working design assumptions**

- Most visitors will arrive with a practical question and limited patience.
- The site should feel local, dependable, and well cared for rather than corporate or luxurious.
- Visit planning should take priority over brand storytelling.

These are planning assumptions, not claims about the business.

**Unknown content that must be supplied or verified**

- Business name, logo, address, phone number, and contact preference
- Opening hours, holiday hours, and last-wash policy
- Machine types and capacities
- Prices and accepted payment methods
- Self-service, wash-and-fold, pickup, delivery, or other offered services
- Detergent and vending availability
- Parking, transit, entrances, seating, restroom, and accessibility details
- Any rules, guarantees, staff assistance, or neighborhood history
- Whether machine availability is available from a real data source; the plan does not assume live status
- Real photographs and permission to use them

## 2. Visitor jobs

The first screen should let a visitor quickly:

1. Confirm where the laundromat is and when it is open once those facts are supplied.
2. Get directions or contact the business once those details are supplied.
3. Learn what services, machines, prices, and payment methods actually apply.
4. Know what to bring and what to expect on a first visit.

Secondary goals are to establish trust, show that the space is cared for, and give the laundromat a recognizable neighborhood character.

## 3. Domain findings

- **Functional truth:** laundry moves from mixed, uncertain, and unfinished to sorted, cared for, and ready to rejoin daily life.
- **Visitor reality:** customers often need a few precise answers before carrying a heavy load across the neighborhood.
- **Human ritual:** laundry is recurring maintenance—a weekly reset that creates a quiet sense of readiness.
- **Material behavior:** fabric gathers, tumbles, folds, stacks, and settles; enamel, stainless steel, baskets, labels, and folding tables belong to the environment.
- **Cultural tension:** laundromats are often presented as either purely transactional or nostalgically stylized, while the actual value is dependable care embedded in ordinary neighborhood life.
- **Native language:** load, cycle, wash, dry, fold, last wash, capacity, detergent, and ready.

The obvious direction—large animated bubbles, spinning washer doors, and bright aqua gradients—is quarantined. It is recognizable but says little about this particular customer experience.

## 4. Directions considered

| Direction | Governing idea | Structure and signature behavior | Main drawback |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The Weekly Reset** | The laundromat is the small neighborhood ritual that gets life ready for the week. | A utility-led page whose information begins as a few practical choices and settles into an orderly personal visit plan. | It needs authentic photography and copy to avoid feeling overly polished. |
| **The Load Ticket** | The site behaves like a clear, oversized care label for the whole visit. | Dense but legible utility, with information grouped by load, machine, payment, and timing. | Highly useful, but could feel clinical and underplay the human side of the business. |

**Selection:** The Weekly Reset is strongest because the practical act of planning a visit and the emotional idea of getting life back in order reinforce one another. The Load Ticket is the strongest rejected alternative, but its administrative tone is less welcoming. The winner's main creative risk is generic warmth: it will only feel specific if real imagery, actual operating details, and the business's own voice replace placeholders.

All four candidates pass the factual-integrity and plan-boundary gates as concepts. The Weekly Reset provides the clearest utility floor and the most credible touch, keyboard, and reduced-motion equivalents.

## 5. Selected concept contract

### Governing concept

**A small weekly reset: the site turns the uncertainty of a laundry trip into a calm, ready-to-go plan.**

### Experience promise

Within seconds, a visitor should understand how to plan a visit once the real business details are supplied. The experience should leave them feeling that the laundromat is straightforward, cared for, and part of ordinary neighborhood life.

### Structure

Use a **hybrid utility journey**: essential visit information remains immediate and persistent around one expressive interaction. The site is a useful local-business page first and a brand experience second.

### Composition

- Build the page like a generous folding surface: broad horizontal work areas, tidy stacks, crisp alignment, and comfortable but not oversized spacing.
- Contrast moments of loose, human texture with finished rows of practical information.
- Keep the first screen compact enough that hours, location, and the main actions are not pushed below a decorative introduction.
- Use rules, small labels, and folded-edge framing sparingly. Avoid a grid of interchangeable cards.

### Typography

- Use a sturdy, friendly sans-serif voice with open shapes and excellent legibility.
- Give hours, prices, capacities, and policies a stable tabular treatment once verified.
- Pair large, plainspoken statements with concise utility labels; avoid cute laundry puns in essential instructions.
- Preserve comfortable reading sizes and obvious hierarchy on small screens.

### Material and color

- Begin with warm cotton white, deep workwear blue, and stainless-steel gray.
- Reserve one signal color—provisionally a safety orange—for open actions, directional cues, and important notices. Final color must be checked against the actual storefront and identity.
- Use fabric grain, enamel edges, and paper-label details as quiet material cues, not decorative overlays on every section.
- Avoid predictable turquoise bubbles, glossy appliance renders, and luxury-cleaning minimalism.

### Imagery

- Prioritize real documentary photographs: the storefront in neighborhood context, the actual room, clear machine views, folding surfaces, useful amenities, and human hands in the process of sorting or folding.
- Faces should appear only with permission. The experience can remain warm through evidence of use without staging customers.
- Do not use generic stock families, invented testimonials, or images that imply an amenity or service that has not been verified.
- If photography is initially unavailable, use strong type, honest information, and a small set of close material studies rather than fake documentary imagery.

### Motion law

**Everything moves from loose to settled.** Information may arrive with a slight sense of gathering, then align and rest decisively. Nothing should spin endlessly or mimic a washing machine merely for effect. Reduced-motion visitors receive the same information already settled in its final order.

### Signature interaction: “Fold to what you need”

At the top of the page, the visitor chooses one of three plain-language intentions:

- **Plan a visit**
- **See what is offered** — content remains explicitly unconfirmed until the business supplies its real services
- **Contact the laundromat** — action remains a placeholder until a verified contact method is supplied

The page then brings the relevant facts together into a short, ordered summary while keeping the full page and primary navigation available. The behavior expresses the concept: a loose set of questions becomes a ready-to-use plan. It is not a gate, quiz, or forced sequence, and choosing again restores a different summary immediately.

### Sound

No sound. Machine noise is part of the physical place, but adding it would make a practical website less comfortable and less discreet.

### Restraint

Deliberately exclude a custom cursor, 3D washer, bubble particles, continuous spinning, cinematic scroll locking, autoplay media, invented live machine counts, and multiple competing interactions. One organizing gesture is enough.

## 6. Experience architecture

### Persistent utility

A compact utility bar should remain easy to reach and contain the verified address, posted hours, directions action, and contact action. On mobile, the most useful two verified actions should remain thumb-reachable without obscuring content. No “open now” claim should appear unless it can be derived reliably from verified hours and exceptions.

### Section sequence

1. **Ready for the week**  
   A concise introduction, the verified business name, the “Fold to what you need” choices, and immediate visit essentials. Address, hours, and contact remain labelled content placeholders until supplied.

2. **Plan your visit**  
   Verified location, posted hours, last-wash policy, directions, and any confirmed parking, transit, or entrance guidance. Every operational item remains omitted or visibly marked for confirmation until verified.

3. **Choose the right load**  
   A clear inventory of only the verified services, machine capacities, prices, cycle guidance, and accepted payments. If the business offers self-service only, this section should say that plainly rather than imply broader services.

4. **Your first visit**  
   A short checklist based solely on confirmed facts: what to bring, what is available on site, how payment works, and where staff help can be found if staff assistance is verified.

5. **The neighborhood room**  
   Real photographs and a brief, factual note about the business or place. This section earns trust through specificity, not an invented origin story or unsupported community claims.

6. **Ready when you are**  
   A compact repeat of verified hours, directions, and contact details, plus any important confirmed policy. No newsletter, account, or booking flow is assumed.

### Responsive expression

- On desktop, information can gather side by side like sorted stacks on a folding table.
- On touch screens, the same stacks become a clear vertical sequence; the signature choices act as large, direct controls rather than relying on hover.
- The utility bar should condense to the two most important verified actions, with every other detail still available nearby.
- Photography should crop around real operational evidence—entrance, machines, signage—not around decorative atmosphere alone.

### Accessibility expression

- All information and choices must be reachable in a logical keyboard order with visible focus.
- The signature interaction changes emphasis, not availability; the full content remains readable without using it.
- Each choice communicates its selected state in text and does not depend on color or movement.
- Reduced-motion presentation begins in the final settled layout with no loss of meaning.
- Plain labels, strong contrast, semantic headings, and concise directions take precedence over clever phrasing.
- Location and contact details should appear as text as well as actions so they can be copied or read with assistive technology.

### What stays deliberately simple

- Navigation remains short and conventional.
- Operational details use plain lists and tables where those are clearest.
- Directions hand off to a familiar map action; the plan does not assume a custom map.
- There is no account, booking, live status, or payment experience unless a future verified business requirement introduces one.

## 7. Content and asset checklist before design begins

1. Confirm all unknown operational details in the truth ledger.
2. Decide the business's preferred primary contact and directions actions.
3. Photograph the real storefront, entrance, room, machines, folding area, signage, and confirmed amenities.
4. Gather the existing logo, storefront colors, signs, printed price sheets, and care instructions.
5. Identify any accessibility or arrival guidance that customers need before visiting.
6. Ask the owner for a few phrases they naturally use with customers; these should shape the voice more than generic marketing copy.

## 9. Constraint audit

- **Reference compliance:** Pass. The plan diverges across distinct concept families, selects one with the required rubric, retains a creative risk, and applies both receipt constraints.
- **Factual integrity:** Pass. No business name, hours, price, service, amenity, policy, history, integration, or live capability is presented as fact without being supplied; operational unknowns remain marked where they would appear.
- **Utility:** Pass. Visit information and core actions remain immediate, persistent, and available without the signature interaction.
- **Accessibility:** Pass at plan level. Keyboard, touch, reduced-motion, and non-interactive equivalents preserve the concept and content.
- **Boundary compliance:** Pass. The plan ends at experience architecture and contains no stack, dependency list, runtime design, performance budget, or build sequence.
